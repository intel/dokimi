### Fixed

- **Sign-flip interval of a Monte Carlo row whose deltas span more than a float sum resolves** — for a row of more than 16 changed deltas, `sign_flip_interval` counted each shift's resamples with float sums, so the small deltas of `[1e16] + [1.0] * 19` were absorbed by the large one and its lower end came out as 0.9474 (0.0001 for `1e20` or `1e300`) while the exact p puts it at 1.0. At each shift it tests, when the shifted deltas span more than 40 binary orders of magnitude, the resamples are now counted on exact integer sums, with the same draws, so `p(0)` still equals `sign_flip_p` for the seed. A row whose shifted deltas span 40 orders or fewer at every shift the search tests, and any row of 16 deltas or fewer, gives the same ends as before; a row that spans about 40 at 0 can cross the line as the shift moves, and its lower end then changes (`[2.0 ** 40] + [1.0] * 19`: 1.0, not 0.9999969). The exact sums make such a row slower to search: about 0.2 s for `1e16`, about 4 s for `1e300`, and about 160 s for `1e300` with 999 deltas of about `1e-8`.
