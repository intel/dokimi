## Rationale
A cap that takes atoms in document order measures the opening of the input, not the input. At `--tier triage` on the dpnp repository's agent guidance, the 25 capped atoms of the root `AGENTS.md` (61 spans) came from spans s0002 to s0012, about a quarter of its tokens, and those of `dpnp/tests/AGENTS.md` (32 spans) from s0003 to s0013, about a third. Two artifacts' capped rates then describe different shares of their text, and a file's rate moves when its sections are reordered.

A capped run now extracts every span and, of the M atoms extracted, probes the n at indices `floor((i + 0.5) * M / n)`, in document order (on a bundle run, in file order). A per-atom stride estimates the same per-atom rate an uncapped run reports. Equal token strata would not: they would weight a sparse introduction like a dense rule list. The stride needs no seed, so the same text gives the same sample. Probing stays at the cap; extraction grows to every batch of the input.

The tier profiles (the call and token figures `--estimate-only` is fitted and checked on, and the per-tier call counts in `docs/skill-novelty.md`) were measured with the document-order cap. Only artifacts with more atoms than a tier's cap (25 at triage, 60 at standard, 120 at study) are affected; below the cap every atom is probed, as before. Re-measuring those artifacts, and refitting the extraction profile to a run that asks every batch, is follow-up work this decision does not do.
