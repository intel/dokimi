## Decision
A paired comparison over tasks reports, as its 95% interval, every shift theta at which its two-sided sign-flip test on the per-task deltas minus theta does not reject at 5%, uncorrected. Each end is found by bisection between the mean and a far bound. The test is exact up to 16 non-zero shifted deltas and Monte Carlo above, with the draws made once from the fixed seed and reused at every theta. The interval then excludes 0 exactly when the row's p is below 0.05. This holds for every measure: no percentile bootstrap, no t interval. `repo compare` does this from this decision on, and each `--json` row names its method (`delta.method` `sign_flip_inversion`). The arms track (`dokimi/eval/stats.py`) does it too, from issue #421 on: `arms run` per arm and per interaction contrast, `report model-compare` and the with/without eval report take `p` from the sign-flip test on the per-question deltas and their interval from inverting it (`ci_method` `sign_flip_inversion`), with `n_changed` the pairs whose delta is not 0 and `significant` null where the floor 2/2^k over those k pairs is not below 0.05. Both tracks import the test from `dokimi/eval/sign_flip.py`.

## Rationale
`repo compare` took a row's interval from one method and its p from another, a percentile bootstrap for gate and score and a t interval for the other measures next to a sign-flip p, and near 0.05 the two disagreed. On a replay of a dpnp PR (26 `review_replay` tasks), `metric:n_findings` read +0.89 with a t interval of [-0.091, +1.860] across 0, against a sign-flip p of 0.0497 (the t test gave 0.074). A score row of 6 tasks read +0.139 with a bootstrap interval of [+0.028, +0.250] clear of 0, against p 0.25: three non-zero deltas, all positive, cannot give an exact sign-flip p below 0.25. The `interval_p_disagree` note flagged such rows; inverting the test removes the cause. On deltas that reproduce those rows, the intervals are now [+0.059, +1.818] and [0.000, +0.333].

- **Holm.** Gate and score rows are decided by Holm's `p_holm`, and Holm's step-down has no simple compatible interval: the 0.05/m level is compatible with Bonferroni, not with Holm. The interval is inverted at the uncorrected 0.05, and says so. In a family of 2+ rows it can still exclude 0 where Holm lifts p across 0.05; `interval_p_disagree` stays for that case only, and its note says the interval is uncorrected and `p_holm` decides the row. `mde` and `p_holm_floor` describe p and are unchanged.
- **Ties under a shift.** At theta other than 0 a tied task is no longer tied: its delta becomes -theta. Far from the mean every task counts, and the smallest p is 2/2^n over all n tasks. The interval is therefore bounded on both sides once n >= 6, whatever the ties, and open (`null`) at 5 or fewer tasks. The floor note keeps describing p at theta = 0, where only the changed tasks count.
- **Discreteness.** The test stays exact, with no mid-p: the point is that the interval and the printed p agree, and a mid-p interval would disagree with the exact p a row prints. p(theta) is a step function and the ends are its step points.
- **At 0, and above 16 tasks.** The row's own p decides whether 0 is inside. For more than 16 changed tasks the interval's Monte Carlo signs for the changed tasks are the very draws the printed p uses, so the printed p does not change. A row of more than 16 tasks with 16 or fewer changed has an exact p and a Monte Carlo interval; the row's p places 0 there too.
- **No flag for the old method.** Each row names its method, so old and new output cannot be confused, and the release before this change still redoes a comparison the old way. A flag would double the tests of a module at its line cap.
- **The arms track.** Its verdict needed both a t test and a Wilcoxon test below 0.05, next to a Student t interval, so the interval agreed with the t test and not with the verdict. Both tests are dropped rather than kept beside the sign-flip p, which would bring the split back (F020: the old rule ran at about 3% false positives at a nominal 5%; verdicts near 0.05 move, almost always toward significant). The 6-pair gate becomes the floor rule, which counts changed pairs: 20 pairs with 15 ties cannot reach 0.05, while 2 to 5 pairs now get a p and no verdict. A constant shift gets a p and a verdict (eight +20 deltas: 0.0078, interval [+20, +20]); only Cohen's d_z stays null there. Holm or BH corrects `p` into `p_adjusted` across the arms, and across the contrasts as a family of its own; an arm under the floor stays in the family, as a `repo compare` row does, and the interval stays uncorrected. The contrast test assumes `AB - A - B + baseline` symmetric about 0 under no interaction, which independent judge noise in the four cells gives.
- **Speed.** One 2^k x k sign matrix per k, built once, with one matrix-vector product per theta. A local run timed one `paired_summary` row: the interval took about 4 ms at 16 changed tasks (2^16 patterns), 2 ms at 26 tasks and 13 ms at 200 tasks (Monte Carlo, 20 000 draws). Those are a tenth or less of the time the sign-flip p itself takes on the same row (48, 20 and 149 ms).
